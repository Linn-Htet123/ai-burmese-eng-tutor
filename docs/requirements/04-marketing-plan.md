## 1. Positioning

**One line:** Interview-ready in 4 weeks. Then keep going. Daily 40-minute practice with a tutor who upgrades the way you already speak, in Burmese and English. $25/month, cancel anytime.

**Open and start, no booking.** Worth putting in the advertisement itself, because it is the clearest difference from a human tutor and it removes the objection people do not say out loud: that arranging a lesson is a hassle and speaking badly to a stranger is worse.

**What it is not:** not a grammar course, not an accent course, not another app you will stop opening.

### The positioning statement that does the work

> Your English isn't wrong. It's just not smooth yet.

This is the whole pitch. It is accurate, it flatters the buyer honestly rather than falsely, and it separates the product from every "learn English" advertisement the buyer has already scrolled past. Every piece of copy should be traceable back to it.

### Who to say no to

Absolute beginners. "Some English already" has to be in the advertisement. Selling to someone with no English produces refunds, bad completion numbers, and reviews describing a product this is not.

Note the nuance, because it will come up in the comments: the product does have low levels and it does teach grammar. What it cannot serve is someone starting from nothing. The line in the copy is "you can already say some things in English, you just cannot say them smoothly or fast enough."

### On mentioning levels in marketing

Do not lead with the level system. It is a strong product feature and a weak advertisement, because it invites people to self-assess before they have tried anything, and most people guess their level wrong and low. Lead with the problem and the outcome. Levels get explained after the free session, when the placement has already told them where they are.

## 2. The proof asset

Same as the kids product, and it remains the single most important marketing decision.

**Session 1 recording, next to session 12 recording. Same person, same question.**

That comparison sells the product better than any copy, and it cannot be faked, which is exactly why it works in a market that has been sold a lot of English courses.

Requirements:
* Capture session 1 audio for **every** learner from day one. Retroactive is impossible.
* Two separate consents: recording for their own use, and permission to use it publicly. Ask for the second one at session 12, when they are pleased, not at signup, when they are cautious.
* 30 to 45 seconds each side. Longer does not help.

⚠️ There is no proof asset for four weeks after launch. Plan the first month's marketing without it.

## 3. Channels

Ranked by likely return, and all of it is estimate rather than evidence.

### Primary: Burmese job and career groups on Facebook

Facebook is where the Burmese internet is. The kids plan targeted parent groups. Here the equivalent is **job seeking, career, study abroad, and expat worker groups**, in Myanmar and in Thailand.

Rules, carried from the kids plan because they were right:
* Participate before selling. Answer interview questions for free for two or three weeks first.
* Post value, not advertisements. "Three English phrases that buy you thinking time in an interview" gets shared. "Sign up for my course" gets removed by an admin.
* The founder's own story is the asset. A Burmese technical founder who stalls translating and built the thing he needed is a far better post than any product description.

### Secondary: the diaspora, especially Thailand

Burmese professionals in Thailand have better payment rails, higher incomes, and a sharper daily need for English at work. Smaller population, much higher conversion likelihood.

This may in fact be the correct beachhead rather than the secondary channel. It is listed second only because Myanmar is where the founder's network is. Worth testing both in the first month and letting the data decide.

### Tertiary: referral, once there are graduates

Adults who finish something tell people. Build the referral ask into session 12, when the before and after clip has just been played back to them. That is the highest-intent moment in the entire product.

### Later: LinkedIn, TikTok, employer partnerships

Not for launch.

### Explicitly not doing

* Paid ads before there is a working funnel. Money spent learning what a landing page could have taught for free.
* Language schools and tuition centres. Same conclusion as the kids product: they copy rather than partner.
* App store optimisation. Nobody searches their way to this.

## 4. Pricing and packaging

Price is **$25/month subscription** (D-042). Packaging framing is what marketing needs to nail.

**Sell the pack, not the subscription.**

Under D-042 the product is a monthly subscription, but marketing surfaces the **first-month pack** (D-043) — the interview-ready-in-4-weeks arc — because "$25/month for daily English practice" is a generic pitch in a way that "interview-ready in 4 weeks" is not. The pack is the story; the subscription is the delivery mechanism.

Frame against tutoring, which remains the honest comparison. ~7-9 hours of daily practice across the first month is comparable to the same hours of private tutoring at $8-25/hr on italki, but for $25 flat. Framed that way the product looks cheap rather than automated.

### Packaging rules

* One price, one product. No tiers at launch.
* No free trial of the full subscription. A **free first session** is fine and doubles as placement.
* First-billing-cycle refund policy TBD (see PRD R-PY-6 / O-10). Whatever it is, say it plainly in the copy — buyers who have been disappointed by courses before need this stated up front.
* Cancel-anytime must be visible next to the price. Removes the "am I signing up for a year?" objection before it forms.

## 5. Trust

The real barrier, and different from the kids product.

For parents the barrier was safety. For adults it is **embarrassment**. This buyer has been told their English is bad before, probably at school, and speaking badly in front of anyone is precisely what they avoid. That avoidance is why they still cannot speak fluently.

An AI tutor is an advantage here, and the marketing should say so directly: nobody is watching, nobody is judging, you can be bad at this in private until you are not.

Second barrier: they have bought English courses before and not finished them. Address it head-on. The **pack-shaped first month** — a real week-4 milestone with a before/after clip, inside a subscription they can cancel anytime — is the answer (D-042, D-043). Say that in the copy rather than hoping they notice.

## 6. Launch sequence

**Weeks 1 to 2, before the product works**
* Ten pricing interviews. Existing spend, not hypothetical willingness.
* Join five to eight Burmese career and job groups. Answer questions. Sell nothing.
* One landing page, one price, one email field. Measure whether anyone signs up before anything is built.

**Weeks 3 to 4**
* Founder story post in two or three groups.
* Free first session offered to ten people from those groups. This is the placement data, the content test, and the first cohort.

**Weeks 5 to 8, first paid cohort**
* Ten to fifteen paying learners, run personally.
* Capture every session 1 recording.
* Watch the drop-off curve by session number. This is the only number that matters.

**Week 9 onward**
* First before and after clips exist. Marketing changes completely at this point.
* Referral ask built into session 12.
* Only now consider paid acquisition.

## 7. Metrics

| Stage | Metric | Target |
|---|---|---|
| Awareness | Landing page visits from group posts | Directional only |
| Interest | Email signups per post | 5%+ of visits |
| Trial | Free first session booked | 30% of signups |
| Conversion | Free session to paid subscription | 25%+ |
| **Retention (month 1)** | **Reach the week-4 before/after clip** | **50%+ for cohort 1** |
| **Retention (month 2)** | **Renew for month 2 after the week-4 clip** | **40%+ of month-1 completers** |
| Referral | Graduates who refer someone | 20% |
| Revenue | Active paid subscribers | 8 by month 4, the $200/month target ($25/mo × 8) |

Everything except completion is a guess. Completion is the number the business lives on.

## 8. Messaging

**Headline options, to test:**
* Your English isn't wrong. It's just not smooth yet.
* You know what you want to say. Say it faster.
* Interview-ready in 4 weeks. Daily practice with May. $25/month.

**Body, the shape of it:**

You think in Burmese. You translate. By the time the English is ready, the conversation has moved on and you said nothing, or something smaller than you meant.

That isn't a grammar problem. It's speed. Speed only comes from speaking, and speaking is the one thing every course you've bought didn't make you do.

May asks you real interview questions. You answer however you can. She tells you what worked, then gives you the version a native speaker would use, and you say it back. Daily 40-minute practice. Interview-ready in 4 weeks. $25/month, in Burmese and English, cancel anytime.

**What to avoid:**
* "Fluent in 30 days". Nobody believes it and it attracts the wrong buyer.
* "AI-powered". The buyer does not care and it invites comparison to free chatbots.
* Anything implying their current English is bad. The entire positioning is the opposite.
* Stock photos of Western people in offices.
